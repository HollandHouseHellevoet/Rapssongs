LEAD DOSSIER · WAYS & MEANS

Inside the markup: how HR 4183 quietly rewrote the R&D credit.

A fiscal footnote the lobbyists wrote themselves — reconstructed from committee drafts, whip counts, and three dozen filings.

BY ROJAS DESK WITH WAYS & MEANS · POH FILED 13 APR 2026 · 06:55 ET UPDATED 13 APR · 11:04 ET

The committee print arrived at 4:07 a.m. — twenty‑six minutes after the staff director sent the second whip count, ninety‑four minutes before the markup was scheduled to gavel. By the time members read it, the language had been on a lobbyist's hard drive for nine months.

None of that is new. What's new is footnote 14, on page 311 of a 412‑page bill, which redefines "qualified research" so quietly that the Joint Committee on Taxation needed three days to score it. By then, the markup had cleared subcommittee on a 24‑18 vote.

This dossier reconstructs how the footnote got there: who wrote it, who walked it through, who paid attention, and who was told not to.

Who wrote the language

The earliest verbatim string of footnote 14 appears in a March 2025 white paper from a coalition of seven biotech firms, filed as part of a comment letter to Treasury. The string — twenty‑six words, lightly rearranged — moves through three industry letters and a Senate Finance staff memo before it surfaces in the committee print.

"We have a saying in this room: nothing is in the bill until it's in the bill. Then it was in the bill." — senior committee staffer, granted anonymity to discuss the markup
